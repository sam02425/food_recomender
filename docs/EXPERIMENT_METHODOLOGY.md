# Experimental Methodology: Emotion-Responsive Food Ordering Systems

## Overview

This document provides comprehensive methodological details for conducting controlled experiments comparing baseline and emotion-responsive food ordering interfaces. The methodology has been validated through a completed study with 50 participants and 500 total trials.

## Experimental Design

### Study Type
- **Design**: Within-subjects controlled experiment
- **Conditions**: 2 (Baseline vs Emotion-Responsive)
- **Counterbalancing**: Latin square design to control for order effects
- **Blinding**: Participants unaware of specific research hypotheses

### Independent Variables

#### Condition A: Baseline Interface
- Static menu presentation
- No personalization features
- Minimal system recommendations
- Standard visual design
- Required selection of "Experiment A Baseline" button for condition verification

#### Condition B: Emotion-Responsive Interface
- Real-time facial emotion detection
- Multi-agent recommendation system
- Adaptive interface elements
- Contextual health and weather integration
- Personalized user experience

### Dependent Variables

#### Primary Measures
1. **Task Completion Time** (seconds)
2. **User Satisfaction** (7-point Likert scale)
3. **Cognitive Workload** (NASA-TLX)
4. **System Usability** (SUS Scale)
5. **Recommendation Acceptance Rate** (percentage)

#### Secondary Measures
1. **Navigation Efficiency** (menu steps)
2. **Error Frequency** (corrections per trial)
3. **Decision Changes** (modifications during ordering)
4. **Trust and Confidence** (7-point scales)
5. **Emotional Engagement** (qualitative assessment)

## Participant Requirements

### Inclusion Criteria
- Age: 18-65 years
- Regular experience with digital food ordering (≥2 times/month)
- Normal or corrected-to-normal vision
- Informed consent for facial recognition and data collection
- English proficiency for interface interaction

### Exclusion Criteria
- Severe food allergies requiring specialized ordering procedures
- Visual impairments affecting interface interaction
- Previous experience with the experimental system
- Pregnancy (if studying health recommendations)

### Sample Size Calculation
- **Effect Size**: Cohen's d = 0.5 (medium effect)
- **Power**: 0.80
- **Alpha Level**: 0.05
- **Required Sample**: 34 participants
- **Recommended Sample**: 50 participants (allowing for 15% attrition)

## Experimental Procedure

### Session Timeline (90 minutes total)

#### Phase 1: Setup and Consent (15 minutes)
1. **Informed Consent** (5 minutes)
   - Explain facial recognition procedures
   - Clarify data usage and privacy
   - Obtain signatures and permissions

2. **Demographics and Baseline** (5 minutes)
   - Age, gender, technical proficiency
   - Food ordering experience
   - Dietary preferences and restrictions

3. **System Orientation** (5 minutes)
   - Brief interface demonstration
   - Practice trial (not recorded)
   - Calibrate face recognition system

#### Phase 2: Condition A (25 minutes)
1. **Pre-Condition Setup** (2 minutes)
   - Condition assignment based on counterbalancing
   - System configuration for baseline mode

2. **Trial Execution** (20 minutes)
   - 5 ordering trials (4 minutes each)
   - Immediate post-trial assessments
   - Short breaks between trials

3. **Post-Condition Assessment** (3 minutes)
   - SUS questionnaire
   - Condition-specific questions

#### Phase 3: Rest Period (10 minutes)
- Comfort break
- Prevent fatigue effects
- Informal discussion (not recorded)

#### Phase 4: Condition B (25 minutes)
1. **Pre-Condition Setup** (2 minutes)
   - System reconfiguration
   - Face recognition calibration

2. **Trial Execution** (20 minutes)
   - 5 ordering trials (4 minutes each)
   - Immediate post-trial assessments
   - Short breaks between trials

3. **Post-Condition Assessment** (3 minutes)
   - SUS questionnaire
   - Condition-specific questions

#### Phase 5: Final Assessment (15 minutes)
1. **Comparative Evaluation** (5 minutes)
   - Direct condition comparison
   - Preference rankings
   - Overall satisfaction

2. **Semi-Structured Interview** (8 minutes)
   - Experience descriptions
   - Improvement suggestions
   - Trust and privacy concerns

3. **Debriefing** (2 minutes)
   - Study purpose explanation
   - Questions and concerns
   - Future contact information

## Trial Structure

### Individual Trial Procedure (4 minutes each)

#### Pre-Trial (30 seconds)
1. **Condition Verification**
   - Confirm correct interface mode
   - Check system functionality

2. **Context Setting** (Condition B only)
   - Activity level input
   - Health goals specification
   - Weather acknowledgment

#### Task Execution (3 minutes)
1. **Order Scenario Presentation**
   - Scenario type: Free choice (3 trials) or Specific requirement (2 trials)
   - Example scenarios: "Healthy lunch after workout", "Comfort food for study session"

2. **Interface Interaction**
   - Menu navigation and selection
   - Recommendation engagement (Condition B)
   - Order customization and completion

3. **Data Collection**
   - Automatic logging of all interactions
   - Timing measurements
   - Click/navigation tracking

#### Post-Trial (30 seconds)
1. **Immediate Assessment**
   - NASA-TLX (simplified version)
   - Satisfaction rating
   - Difficulty perception

## Statistical Analysis Plan

### Primary Analyses
1. **Paired t-tests**
   - Compare means between conditions
   - Calculate effect sizes (Cohen's d)
   - Test significance at α = 0.05

2. **Repeated Measures ANOVA**
   - Examine learning effects within conditions
   - Test condition × trial interactions
   - Control for individual differences

### Secondary Analyses
1. **Correlation Analyses**
   - Objective vs subjective measures
   - Individual difference predictors
   - Cross-measure relationships

2. **Qualitative Analysis**
   - Thematic analysis of interviews
   - Content analysis of open responses
   - Mixed-methods integration

### Power Analysis Verification
- Post-hoc power calculations
- Effect size confidence intervals
- Sample adequacy assessment
